👁 Visual Impairment Strategies

Practical methods for teaching learners who are blind or have low vision

Braille

Use Braille books, slate & stylus. Begin with letters, then words.

Tactile Materials

Sandpaper letters, raised maps, real objects for concrete learning.

Audio Learning

Recorded lessons, talking books, oral instructions and discussions.

Large Print

Use 18pt+ fonts, high contrast (black on yellow), magnifiers.

Orientation & Mobility

Teach white cane use, sighted-guide techniques, room layouts.

Peer Support

Pair with a sighted buddy for note sharing and group work.

👂 Hearing Impairment Strategies

Methods for deaf and hard-of-hearing learners

Sign Language

Use Zambian Sign Language (ZSL); learn classroom signs.

Lip Reading

Face the learner, speak clearly, avoid covering your mouth.

Visual Aids

Pictures, charts, written instructions, captioned video.

Seating

Front-row, away from noise, with clear view of teacher.

Total Communication

Combine speech, signs, gestures and writing together.

Hearing Aids

Check daily, reduce background noise, use FM systems.

🧠 Learning Disabilities Strategies

Support for dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD and more

Multi-Sensory

See, hear, touch and do — combine senses to deepen learning.

Chunking

Break tasks into small manageable steps with checklists.

Repetition

Revisit concepts daily, use songs, rhymes and chants.

Visual Schedules

Picture timetables to reduce anxiety and confusion.

Positive Reinforcement

Praise effort, use stickers, certificates and rewards.

Assistive Tech

Calculators, text-to-speech apps, coloured overlays.

⚖ Disability Human Rights & Acts

Essential laws every Zambian teacher should know

🇿🇲 Persons with Disabilities Act No. 6 of 2012

Zambia's main disability law. Guarantees:

  • Right to inclusive education at all levels
  • Reasonable accommodation in schools
  • Protection from discrimination
  • Access to assistive devices
  • Establishes the Zambia Agency for Persons with Disabilities (ZAPD)

🌍 UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)

Zambia ratified in 2010. Key articles:

  • Article 7 — Children with disabilities
  • Article 24 — Inclusive education
  • Article 9 — Accessibility
  • Article 8 — Awareness raising

📜 Constitution of Zambia (Amendment 2016)

  • Article 23 — Protection from discrimination including disability
  • Article 54 — Rights of persons with disabilities
  • Right to dignity, education and equal opportunity

📚 Education Act No. 23 of 2011

  • Free and compulsory basic education for all
  • Special educational needs to be addressed
  • Schools must not refuse a child based on disability

👶 Children's Code Act No. 12 of 2022

  • Special protection for children with disabilities
  • Right to education, health and family life
  • Protection from abuse, neglect & exploitation

🌍 UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC)

  • Article 23 — Children with disabilities have right to special care
  • Article 28 — Right to education
  • Article 2 — No discrimination

🤝 Salamanca Statement (1994)

UNESCO framework calling on schools to accommodate ALL children regardless of physical, intellectual, social or other conditions. Foundation of inclusive education globally.

🎯 Sustainable Development Goal 4

"Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for ALL." Zambia is committed to SDG 4 by 2030.

👩‍🏫 Teacher Responsibilities

  • Respect dignity of every learner
  • Make reasonable accommodations
  • Report abuse or discrimination
  • Involve parents in decisions
  • Maintain confidentiality of learner records
  • Promote acceptance among classmates

🇿🇲 Zambia Special Education

Local context, policies and low-cost ideas

📋 National Policy on Education

"Educating Our Future" (1996) — recognises inclusive education as a fundamental approach for learners with special educational needs.

🏥 Curriculum Development Centre

The CDC provides adapted curricula, Braille materials and guidelines for special schools and units across Zambia.

🌾 Rural School Adaptation

  • Use local languages where possible
  • Engage village heads & parents
  • Adapt timetable to farming season
  • Use community resource persons

💡 Low-Cost Materials

  • Bottle caps for counting
  • Sand trays for writing
  • Old cartons for puzzles
  • Sticks & stones for maths
  • Cloth strips for tactile art
